09Wristbands, cashless and the gate

The short version: The band on your wrist is mostly a number. The chip inside carries a unique ID that the festival looks up in its own database every time you tap or walk through a gate, which is why activating it online matters and why a cut band is a dead band. Many festivals now run the bar, the food trucks and the merch tent off that same chip, and the leftover money comes back only if you ask for it online, inside a window, usually minus a small fee. The gate is where all of this meets a bag check, a scanner and, if you are unlucky, a counterfeit.

Did you know?Visitors to Sziget, VOLT and Balaton Sound in Hungary left more than HUF 104 million unclaimed on their festival cards in 2018.185

What is actually inside a festival wristband?

A small radio chip and an antenna, and the chip's main job is to say one number. Intellitix, a festival RFID vendor, says the chip holds a unique identifier (UID) and "does not hold any personal information"; the scanner reads the UID and checks it against a secure database where your ticket, your name and any payment details live.172 RFID (radio frequency identification) is the umbrella term: no battery, powered by the reader's own radio field when it comes close.

Two radio families show up at festivals, and they do different jobs:

Type Frequency Typical read range What festivals use it for
HF / NFC (near field communication) 13.56 MHz about 4 cm (1.5 in) Deliberate taps: paying at a bar, topping up, VIP lanes
UHF (ultra high frequency) UHF band about 1-6 m (3-20 ft) in standard setups Gates: several bands read at once, timestamped, hands-free
Dual-frequency Both Both One band that does gates and payments

Figures from Wristband.com, a wristband seller (May 2026 guide).173 The short NFC range is a feature: you do not want a bar terminal charging the person behind you. The long UHF range is also a feature: a gate can read a crowd walking through without anyone stopping to tap.

Ticket Fairy, a ticketing vendor, describes the reads as encrypted challenge-response exchanges rather than a plain number being shouted into the air, and says readers and payment terminals can work offline, caching transactions until the network comes back.174 That offline mode matters more than it sounds - see the phone section below.

Do festival wristbands track your location?

According to the vendors and festivals that run them, no. Intellitix says bands are scanned only at marked access points and do not communicate location after entry or exit.172 splash! Festival in Germany says flatly that its chips have no GPS.175 What a band does leave is a trail of scans: every gate you passed and, on a cashless site, every purchase. splash! is candid about this, saying its transactions are recorded and the system is therefore "not fully anonymous."175 So the honest version: a wristband knows where you were each time you tapped it, not where you are.

How do I activate my festival wristband, and why bother?

Activation means registering the band's ID online so the festival's database ties it to you. Hinterland in Iowa, in festival information it says is from its 2026 edition, asks attendees to activate before arriving, link a payment card if they want to pay cashless, and add an emergency contact.176

The practical payoff is the lost band. Because the money and the ticket live in the database rather than on the chip, a registered band can be frozen online, according to Intellitix, and splash! says a registered chip can be blocked and its balance moved to a new one.172175 An unregistered band that falls off in a field is, to the system, a stranger's band with your money on it.

Activation also sets up re-entry rules. Hinterland requires an exit scan for same-day re-entry.176 Ticket Fairy describes the general logic: a gate marks a band "used" on the way in and will not let it in again until it has been scanned out.174 Wristband.com calls this anti-passback - the gate flags a repeat entry with no exit between.173 It is the reason you cannot walk in, hand your band through the fence, and have a friend walk in on it.

Why are some festival wristbands impossible to take off?

Because the lock only goes one way. SeatGeek's explainer says fabric bands have a locking mechanism that cannot be removed without damage, and most use a one-way lock: once fastened, the band cannot be loosened.177 Pull it tighter and that is where it stays. SeatGeek adds the warning every first-timer learns the hard way: tighten it too far and you may have to cut it, which can invalidate it.177

Festivals write this into the terms. Hinterland's wristband policy says the band must be worn so it "cannot be slipped on or off," that removing, stretching or tampering "will void entry," and that the band is a "revocable license" rather than something you own outright.176 Intellitix says a broken-off band cannot be reattached.172

The design goal is one band, one wrist, for the whole weekend. A band that could slide off could be sold on the walk home on Friday night or passed back over a fence. The chip handles the digital side of that problem; the lock handles the physical side.

Inside, one common design is a barrel lock with prongs. ID&C, a UK wristband supplier, says its fabric barrel lock uses "a one-way sliding mechanism with internal prongs that pierce the fabric," so that once fastened the band "can't be removed without cutting."178

What happens if I cut my festival wristband?

You will probably need a new one, and it may cost you. Intellitix says damaged bands are replaced at the box office with photo ID, and a fee may apply.172 Hinterland's policy is specific: one replacement only, $25, and only for the original purchaser showing photo ID.176 Removing the band yourself counts as tampering under that policy and voids entry.176 If a band is too tight to bear, go to the box office or the festival's help desk before reaching for scissors.

How to spot a fake festival wristband (and who gets caught selling them)

The fakes are real, and so are the arrests. The Las Vegas Review-Journal reported two men arrested over a counterfeit wristband scheme at Electric Daisy Carnival in Las Vegas, with bands ordered from an out-of-state supplier and an estimated value of $1 million; charges included obtaining money under false pretenses, attempted forgery and conspiracy.179 In Miami, WSVN 7News reported in March 2025 that a man was arrested for allegedly selling two fake Ultra Music Festival wristbands for $1,200 cash, charged with an organized scheme to defraud.180

The EDC arrests date from the festival's June 2011 edition: the Las Vegas Sun reported them on June 25, 2011, saying the event organizer had alerted police to about $1 million in fake "all-access" wristbands.181

The countermeasure is the chip. A printed copy of a band can look perfect and still fail at the gate, because the scanner is checking for a valid ID in the database, not for the right shade of fabric. Ticket Fairy claims Coachella "virtually eliminated counterfeit tickets at its festival gates" with RFID; that is a vendor claim and not independently verified.174 Festivals back the tech with policy: Hinterland says counterfeit or duplicated bands "will be confiscated by security" and tickets from unauthorized sources will not get in.176

For the buyer, the takeaway is dull and reliable: buy from the festival or its official resale channel. Nobody outside a gate can show you that a band's chip is live.

How does cashless payment work at a festival?

There are two main models, and they handle your money differently.

Model How it works Example
Linked card You attach a real bank card to the band at activation; each tap charges the card Hinterland, Iowa176
Closed-loop You load money onto a festival-only balance or currency; nothing else is accepted splash!, Germany (2025)175; Dour, Belgium (2026)182

At splash! in 2025, the ticket is exchanged on site for an RFID chip that is the only way to pay; the system is run by Weezevent.175 Dour goes a step further with its own currency, the Douro, priced at €1.75 each, with a cap of €425 on a chip.182 Belgium's Tomorrowland also uses RFID wristbands; Ticket Fairy says they once had a button that two people could press at the same time to register as friends.174

A top-up is adding money to the balance. At splash! 2025, topping up online before the festival is free; topping up with cash on site costs €1.50 per transaction, and the balance is capped at €250.175

Why festivals like it: speed and spend, according to vendors. Ticket Fairy claims RFID payments take under 2 seconds against 15-30 seconds for cards (citing payments vendor Billfold), and reports a roughly 24% rise in average spend per attendee at Standon Calling in the UK after it went cashless.174 Both are vendor claims, not independently verified, and no independent study of the spend effect turned up in our research.

The nearest non-vendor source found is thin. A 2019 blog post from the University of St. Gallen's Executive School in Switzerland says sales at Open Air St. Gallen rose after it went cashless in 2013 and that a visitor spends an average of 126 francs, but gives no before-and-after figure.183

How do I get a refund of my cashless festival balance?

Online, after the festival, inside a window, usually minus a fee. Here is what three European festivals published:

Festival Edition Window Fee Other rules
Dour (Belgium) 2026 21 July - 21 August 2026 2 Douros (€3.50) Online only, "no refunds are given on site"; Douros not valid at the next edition182
Boombastic (Spain) 2026 27 July - 14 August 2026 €2.50 Transfers within 20 business days after the window184
splash! (Germany) 2025 No deadline stated €0.50 Minimum balance €1; payout in about 2 weeks175

All figures are official, from each festival's own page. Dour's page dates may carry over from a previous edition, so check the current one.182 Across these samples the fee runs from €0.50 to €3.50.

What happens to money left on a festival wristband?

If you miss the window, it is usually gone. Dour states that "No request will be processed after Friday 21 August 2026" and that remaining credit is lost.182 With a hard deadline, a small fee and a balance of a few euros, many people may not bother.

Money that is paid in and never claimed is sometimes called breakage. Two reported figures put a size on it. Portfolio.hu reported in 2019 that visitors to Sziget, VOLT and Balaton Sound in Hungary left more than HUF 104 million on festival cards in 2018, citing the organizer's company report.185 A University of St. Gallen blog post, citing NZZ am Sonntag, puts unreclaimed balances at 1 to 1.5% of festival credit within two years (2019, reported).183

Two habits help: top up in small amounts, and put the refund deadline in your calendar on the drive home. On the linked-card model there is no balance to forget, because each tap charges your card directly.176

What happens at the festival gate?

Two checks, usually in this order: people and bags, then the band. The band check is the UHF read or NFC tap described above, with anti-passback logic deciding whether you are entering for the first time, re-entering after a proper exit scan, or trying something.173174

The bag check is where most queues form, and the rules are set festival by festival and year by year. US festivals have converged on small bags, sealed or empty water bottles, and bans on things that block views or could be a weapon. Boston Calling's 2023 rules are a fair sample, reported by WBUR:158

Allowed (2023) Not allowed (2023)
Bags smaller than 6 x 9 inches (about 15 x 23 cm) Sleeping bags
Factory-sealed snacks Umbrellas
Hats, sunglasses Chairs
Non-aerosol sunscreen Strollers
One factory-sealed plastic water bottle, or an empty reusable bottle, or an empty hydration pack Instruments, bikes

Read the current FAQ for your festival the week before; sizes and lists change.

UK lists focus more on what is banned than on bag size; Glastonbury's 2025 list, and why bag rules got stricter, are in Crowds, heat and keeping people safe.161

Why is there no phone signal at festivals?

Because a field of 100,000 people is a small city that appears for three days, on land built for a few dozen. Carriers answer with COWs (cells on wheels) - portable cell towers on trucks or trailers - and specialist antennas.

The data shows how hard the load climbs. AT&T reported 29.2 TB of cellular data over Coachella's two weekends in 2016 (18.6 TB the first, 10.6 TB the second), up from about 24 TB in 2015; its kit included COWs, in use by 2015, plus "cheese wheel" antennas and a larger "drum set" antenna, and all of its traffic was cellular, no Wi-Fi. AT&T also claimed Coachella data grew 20 times between 2011 and 2015.186

Verizon reported 10.8 TB over two days at Sea.Hear.Now in Asbury Park, New Jersey, in 2022, up 23% on 2021, with two COWs on site and 5G use up 166%, from 895 GB to 2.4 TB.187

Glastonbury is bigger again. Ticket Fairy puts 2024 traffic at about 225 TB (citing Subtonomy) and, citing analysts CCS Insight, lists temporary masts or sites from all four UK networks: Vodafone 10, EE 9, Virgin Media O2 8, Three 6. It describes COW masts up to about 80 ft (24 m) and rental from a few thousand to tens of thousands of dollars a week.188 That is a secondary vendor source; we could not load the CCS Insight original.

The same source advises organizers to separate staff and production Wi-Fi from public Wi-Fi, to prioritize emergency, staff and payment traffic, and to give payment terminals an offline fallback.188 That last point is why cashless systems cache transactions: the bar keeps serving when the network does not.174 For the radio side of how a tower handles a crowd, see https://airwaves.cold.fyi/cellular/.

In Boston

Boston Calling went cashless in 2023, with wristbands serving as both ticket and payment, WBUR reported that year.158 The bag and bottle rules in the gate table above come from the same 2023 guide; later editions may differ, so check the festival's current FAQ.158 The festival's history and its 2027 plans are in New England.

Elsewhere in the world

Europe leans toward closed-loop systems in which the chip is the only wallet on site: splash! in Germany and Dour in Belgium accept neither cash nor card at the bar, and both publish refund rules with fees and, at Dour, a hard deadline.175182 Spain's Boombastic runs the same pattern with a 2026 refund window and a €2.50 fee.184 Many US festivals instead link a real card at activation, as Hinterland does, which avoids a stored balance altogether.176 Tomorrowland in Belgium also uses RFID wristbands (vendor-reported).174

Quick answers

How do festival RFID wristbands work?

The chip in the band carries a unique ID number that readers look up in the festival's database at gates and bars. NFC chips read at about 4 cm for payments, and UHF chips read at about 1-6 m for fast gate entry, according to vendor guides.

Do festival wristbands track your location?

Vendors and festivals say no: bands are read only at scanners, and splash! Festival says its chips have no GPS. They do leave a record of each gate scan and, on cashless sites, each purchase.

Can you take a festival wristband off and put it back on?

No. Most fabric bands use a one-way lock that tightens but cannot loosen, and festivals such as Hinterland (2026) say removing or tampering with a band voids entry.

What happens if I cut my festival wristband?

It is usually void, and you need a replacement at the box office with photo ID. Hinterland's 2026 policy allows one replacement for $25, for the original purchaser only.

How do I get a refund of my cashless festival balance?

Request it online after the festival, inside the published window. Fees in 2025-2026 examples ran from 0.50 euros at splash! to 3.50 euros at Dour, and Dour says unclaimed credit after its deadline is lost.
